What the KRA Stock Management Notice Says

The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) has issued a public notice on the implementation of the Stock Management Functionality for Electronic Invoicing. It matters most to businesses that use TIMS/eTIMS and keep physical or electronic stock records.

KRA reminds taxpayers in business that they must keep accurate and up-to-date stock records while using electronic invoicing systems, in accordance with the law. Its notice states that stock records should account for goods as they move through a business, including goods that are:

Purchased or receivedSoldTransferredReturnedAdjustedDisposed of

Electronic invoicing is increasingly connected to a business's wider financial records. Issuing an eTIMS invoice is no longer the whole job. Your purchases, stock movements, sales, accounting records and tax returns should all tell the same financial story.

What Does the New Stock Management Functionality Mean?

At a practical level, stock management is about being able to answer six questions:

  • What came into the business?
  • What was sold?
  • What was transferred?
  • What was returned?
  • What was adjusted?
  • What remains in stock?

For businesses that deal in goods, these questions are not only about internal management. They are increasingly relevant to tax compliance and to the accuracy of information reported through electronic systems.

Why Should Businesses Pay Attention?

Keeping separate, unconnected records for sales, purchases, stock and tax returns is becoming hard to sustain. A typical business now holds:

  • An accounting system and an inventory or stock system
  • eTIMS records, VAT records and bank records
  • Purchase invoices and sales invoices
  • Physical stock and annual financial statements

The information in all of these should be capable of being reconciled. KRA has also stated that income and expenses declared in income tax returns are being validated against information sources including TIMS/eTIMS, withholding income tax and Customs import records. That makes accurate bookkeeping, invoicing and supporting documents more important than before.

The Connection Between Stock, eTIMS and Tax Compliance: A Worked Example

Start simple. A business purchases 100 units of a product, sells 60, returns 10 to the supplier and transfers 5 to another branch. With no other adjustments, the expected balance is:

100 − 60 − 10 − 5 = 25 units

The business should be able to explain how it arrived at those 25 units. Now add an opening balance and an adjustment:

What Stock and Tax Records Should a Business Maintain?

Businesses dealing in goods should keep records that cover the entire movement of goods.

1. Purchase records

  • Supplier and invoice number
  • Date and description of goods
  • Quantity and unit price
  • VAT where applicable
  • Total amount
  • Supplier PIN where applicable
  • eTIMS/TIMS invoice information

2. Sales records

  • Customer and date
  • Product and quantity
  • Selling price
  • VAT treatment where applicable
  • Total sales
  • Electronic invoice details

3. Stock records

  • Opening quantity
  • Purchases and sales
  • Returns and transfers
  • Adjustments and disposals
  • Closing quantity

4. Supporting documents

  • Purchase and sales invoices
  • Delivery notes and goods received notes
  • Stock transfer documents
  • Credit and debit notes
  • Return documents
  • Stock count sheets
  • Adjustment approvals and disposal records

What About Businesses With Multiple Branches?

Branch transfers are easy to overlook. If 50 units move from a Nairobi branch to a Kisumu branch, the business should be able to account for:

  • The reduction of stock at Nairobi
  • The increase of stock at Kisumu
  • The date of transfer
  • The products and quantities transferred
  • The supporting documentation

Without proper documentation, a business can end up with unexplained differences between physical stock, accounting records and electronic records.

Stock Returns and Stock Adjustments: How to Record Them

Customer and supplier returns

Say a customer buys 20 units and later returns 3. The business should not simply add 3 units back to physical stock. There should be a clear trail:

Original sale→Customer return→Stock increase→Accounting / electronic record

Likewise, when goods go back to a supplier, the stock records should show the reduction.

Stock adjustments

Adjustments can arise from operational reasons such as damaged goods, expired goods, spoilage, physical counting differences, theft or loss, incorrect earlier entries and other properly documented causes. An adjustment must never simply disappear from the records. The business should be able to say:

  • What was adjusted, and why
  • Who authorized it
  • When it was made
  • What supporting document exists

eTIMS Is More Than Just Issuing an Invoice

The biggest practical lesson is that eTIMS should not be treated as an invoice-generation tool only. It forms part of the wider information environment around tax administration. A business's financial information flows through several stages, so aim for consistency at every one of them.

What If Your Accounting Records Don't Match eTIMS?

Differences can occur for legitimate reasons. Common causes include:

  • Timing differences: an invoice was issued on one date and transmitted later.
  • Transmission problems: an invoice was not successfully transmitted.
  • Incorrect customer details: the customer's PIN or other details were mistyped.
  • Credit notes and adjustments: accounting was adjusted, but the related electronic record needs matching treatment.
  • Accounting errors: transactions duplicated, omitted or misclassified.
  • Data-entry errors: wrong amounts, dates, PINs or accounts.
  • Imported transactions: imports bring additional documentation and records.

The right response is not to force the numbers to agree. Follow this sequence:

Identify→Investigate→Explain→Document→Correct if necessary

KRA Consultative Engagement Forums on eTIMS Stock Management

According to the public notice, KRA intends to hold consultative engagement forums with the business community and other key stakeholders to support implementation. The forums are expected to commence in September 2026, and KRA is encouraging taxpayers to take part. They are meant to help stakeholders:

  • Understand how the functionality works
  • Seek clarification
  • Share implementation challenges
  • Give feedback and recommend possible enhancements

Tip: Check KRA's official channels for forum dates and venues, and bring specific questions about your own stock and invoicing setup.

What Should Businesses Do Now? A 7-Step Stock and Tax-Record Health Check

Don't wait for a compliance problem before reviewing your records.

  1. Review your stock recordsConfirm they show opening stock, purchases, sales, returns, transfers, adjustments and closing stock.
  2. Review your eTIMS recordsCheck that relevant invoices are properly generated and transmitted.
  3. Review your accounting recordsMake sure they agree with supporting documents.
  4. Conduct a physical stock countCompare physical stock with the stock records.
  5. Investigate differencesDo not ignore unexplained differences.
  6. Document adjustmentsKeep evidence for adjustments, returns, transfers and disposals.
  7. Reconcile before filingCompare Sales → eTIMS → Accounting → VAT, and Purchases → eTIMS → Accounting → VAT.

7 Common Stock Management Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Recording purchases but not stock. A supplier invoice is booked but stock is never updated.
  2. Recording sales without reducing stock. Sales should have a matching effect on inventory where applicable.
  3. Ignoring returns. Customer and supplier returns must be documented.
  4. Making unexplained adjustments. Every material adjustment needs an explanation and evidence.
  5. Ignoring branch transfers. Stock moved between locations must be recorded.
  6. Waiting until year-end. Stock reconciliation should not be an annual exercise.
  7. Treating eTIMS and accounting as separate worlds. Both should explain the same underlying transactions.

How Often Should You Reconcile Your Records?

You do not need to wait for the annual return. A practical routine looks like this:

DailyRecord transactions accurately.
WeeklyReview sales, purchases and electronic invoices.
MonthlyReconcile Sales ↔ eTIMS ↔ VAT ↔ Accounting, and Purchases ↔ eTIMS ↔ VAT ↔ Accounting.
QuarterlyReview tax compliance, stock controls, outstanding balances, internal controls, financial performance, unusual variances and supporting documentation.
AnnuallyPrepare financial statements, income tax returns, tax reconciliations, audit schedules and supporting documentation.

What Documents Should You Keep?

Maintain an organized documentation trail:

Sales

  • Sales invoices
  • eTIMS records
  • Receipts
  • Credit and debit notes

Purchases

  • Supplier invoices
  • Purchase orders
  • Goods received notes
  • Payment records

Stock

  • Stock sheets and count reports
  • Transfer notes
  • Return documents
  • Adjustment and disposal records

Financial records

  • Bank statements
  • Cash records
  • General ledger
  • Trial balance
  • Reconciliation schedules

Tax records

  • VAT returns
  • Income tax returns
  • eTIMS records
  • Withholding tax records
  • Import documentation

Existing compliance guidance also recommends keeping contracts, payment evidence, reconciliation schedules, correspondence and evidence of system errors where applicable.

What if you experience a system problem?

Technical challenges happen. If a system problem affects your ability to comply, document it. Keep screenshots, error messages, dates and times, filing attempts, acknowledgement messages, support tickets, emails, correspondence and the relevant transaction records.

The principle is simple: if a system problem affects your compliance, create an audit trail.

Why Bookkeeping Matters More Than Ever

Electronic systems make good bookkeeping increasingly important. You should be able to say how much you sold and purchased, how much stock you received, sold and hold, what you transferred or returned, why an adjustment was made, and whether your accounting records agree with your tax records. These are compliance questions and management questions at the same time.

Accounting

What happened financially?

Tax compliance

How should those transactions be treated for tax purposes?

Internal controls

How do we prevent errors and irregularities?

Audit

Can the financial information be supported and independently evaluated?

Advisory

What can management do with the information?

Strong records therefore support more than tax filing. They help with management decisions, budgeting, cash-flow management, risk management, audit preparation and business planning.

Don't Wait for a Tax Problem to Discover a Record-Keeping Problem

Electronic tax systems reward businesses that find discrepancies before they become hard to explain. Ask regularly:

  • Do my physical stock records agree with my stock system?
  • Do my stock movements agree with my purchases and sales?
  • Do my sales agree with my eTIMS records?
  • Do my accounting records agree with my tax returns?
  • Can I support my expenses and explain unusual differences?
  • Do I have the documents to support my position?

Frequently Asked Questions: KRA eTIMS Stock Management

What is the KRA stock management functionality?

It is part of the electronic invoicing environment intended to support accurate recording of stock movements while businesses use TIMS/eTIMS.

Who should pay attention to the notice?

Businesses engaged in activities involving goods and maintaining stock records should pay particular attention to the requirements communicated by KRA.

What stock movements should be recorded?

Businesses should account for goods that are purchased or received, sold, transferred, returned, adjusted or otherwise disposed of.

Does stock management only concern physical stock?

The practical objective is to ensure that stock records accurately represent the movement of goods through the business and can be reconciled with the business's supporting records.

What should I do if my records do not agree with eTIMS?

Investigate the difference rather than simply changing one record to match another. Identify it, investigate it, explain it, document it and correct it where necessary.

Should businesses keep supporting documents?

Yes. Maintain appropriate documentation supporting your transactions, stock movements, accounting records and tax positions.

When should businesses start preparing?

Now. Begin reviewing your stock, accounting and electronic invoicing records and identify gaps that require attention.

Final Takeaway

The KRA public notice is a reminder that stock records, electronic invoicing, accounting and tax compliance are becoming increasingly interconnected. The practical response is a system where every significant transaction can be traced, from purchase to tax return:

Purchase→Stock received→Stock record→Sale / transfer / return / adjustment→eTIMS record→Accounting record→Tax return

The goal should not simply be to issue an invoice or file a tax return. It should be to maintain accurate, consistent and supportable business records.
